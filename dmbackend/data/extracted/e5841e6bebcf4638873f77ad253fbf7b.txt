Case Study Brief
This case study examines the ethical and practical challenges faced by Priya, an intern at the Tiruchirappalli
Autonomous Systems Research Lab, when integrating a crop-disease vision model containing a module
licensed strictly for non-commercial use into a commercial drone product by a startup. The group analyzed
the conflicting obligations to original creators, the lab, the startup, and Priya's own career, and explored
responsible actions amid sponsorship pressures, licensing constraints, and tight deadlines. Through
role-based discussions and a collaborative group dialogue, the participants identified key ethical
responsibilities and proposed a balanced approach to address the license conflict without compromising
integrity or stakeholder interests.
Problem Analysis
•   The lab's internal documentation inaccurately described the entire model as reusable, conflicting with
the module's non-commercial license, risking unauthorized commercial use.
•   The startup requires a commercial demonstration within 16 days, but replacing the restricted module
would take six weeks and significant additional funding.
•   Priya faces pressure from the startup's job offer contingent on successful integration, creating a personal
conflict between career interests and ethical obligations.
•   Failure to address the license conflict transparently could expose all parties—creators, lab, startup, and
students—to legal, reputational, and financial risks.

Methodology
•   Role-based ethical analysis with participants representing the faculty lead, startup compliance
representative, and the intern to explore multiple perspectives.
•   Detailed review of licensing terms, project timelines, funding sources, and stakeholder obligations to
identify conflicts and responsibilities.
•   Facilitated group discussion to harmonize individual viewpoints into joint recommendations
emphasizing transparency, compliance, and protection of stakeholders.
•   Application of ethical codes focusing on honesty, intellectual property respect, and balancing
self-interest against institutional and creator rights.
Key Findings
•   The lab holds responsibility for correcting inaccurate documentation and preventing unauthorized
commercial use, regardless of good faith assumptions.
•   The startup must independently verify license permissions and avoid commercial use without explicit
written consent from the original creators.
•   Priya must prioritize professional integrity by disclosing the license conflict, refusing unauthorized
integration, and supporting lawful alternatives despite career pressures.
•   A temporary, clearly labeled research-only demonstration may be ethically permissible if strictly
separated from commercial activities and permitted by the license.
Recommendations
•   Pause any commercial use of the restricted module until obtaining explicit written permission or
developing a fully licensed replacement.
•   Implement transparent documentation and communication procedures involving all parties, including
protecting Priya from pressure or retaliation.
•   Establish clear accountability within the lab for maintaining accurate software licensing records and
review processes before labeling software as reusable.
•   Provide documented commitments from the startup to maintain student stipends and support during
any project delays, alongside independent reporting channels for ethical concerns.

Conclusions
The case underscores the paramount importance of respecting intellectual property rights and maintaining
professional integrity in research collaborations, even under significant commercial and personal pressures.
By prioritizing transparent communication, lawful compliance, and stakeholder protection, the lab, startup,
and Priya can navigate licensing conflicts responsibly. The agreed safeguards and role-based
accountability provide a framework to uphold ethical standards while seeking practical solutions that
preserve funding, project viability, and career opportunities without compromising legal or moral obligations.
